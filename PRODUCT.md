# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Visitors who want to know Caleb as a person, including friends, technical peers, potential collaborators, recruiters, and hiring managers. Some will explore the shelf; others will arrive at a direct link or want quick access to projects, background, or contact information.

## Product Purpose

A personal home on the web, with the professional portfolio as a major part of it. The homepage is an imagined, LUSTIGT-inspired wooden shelf populated with Smiskis and meaningful objects representing Caleb's life, interests, and work. Visitors should understand who Caleb is and what he makes, and feel they have explored something personal.

## Brand Personality

Welcoming, tactile, curious. The experience should feel like a meaningful corner of someone's room: specific, lived in, and a little playful. Character poses and object groupings convey personality. Introductory voice is direct and conversational: "Hey, I'm Caleb. Welcome to my little shelf of things I make and care about."

## Anti-references

- Generic portfolio templates with interchangeable project cards and technology badges as the main identity.
- Busy scenes filled with decorative objects that have no personal meaning.
- Navigation that requires guessing, hovering, solving a puzzle, or learning commands.
- Small reading areas that make longer project descriptions or screenshots difficult to inspect.
- Motion or sound required to access essential content.

## Design Principles

1. Make the objects meaningful. Each main scene connects a personal object or character grouping to something Caleb wants to share.
2. Support exploration and direct access. Scene selection and persistent text navigation lead to the same content, with shareable URLs and working browser history.
3. Give the work space. Selected scenes can remain visible, while projects and longer content receive a spacious reading area.
4. Make the shelf recognizably Caleb's. The framed photo, miniature laptop, Rubik's cube, tennis ball, and collected figures establish the initial composition.
5. Build a comfortable core before adding discoveries. Establish clear arrival, selection, reading, and return states before night lighting, extra animations, hidden interactions, or sound.

## Content Structure

- **About:** A Smiski beside a framed photo. Introduction, background, current activities, experience, education, and resume access.
- **Projects:** Smiskis gathered around a miniature laptop. Selected work, screenshots, contributions, outcomes, and relevant links.
- **Interests:** Smiskis interacting with a Rubik's cube and tennis ball. Cubing, tennis, collecting, and other interests as Caleb supplies them.
- **Contact:** A small envelope and nearby figure. Email and relevant social or professional profiles.

Retain useful existing portfolio data, verify time-sensitive statements, and develop richer project material with Caleb. Do not invent biographical facts, project results, photographs of Caleb, or current roles.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Provide keyboard navigation, visible focus indicators, descriptive scene labels, comfortable tap targets, and reduced-motion support. Essential information must remain available without hover and while visual assets load or advanced rendering is unavailable. Maintain readable text and navigation in both eventual lighting modes. On mobile, adapt the scene arrangement vertically and use full-width content views with clear return controls.

## First Version

Establish one daytime shelf composition, three main scenes, a contact element, persistent text navigation, selection and return states, shareable section URLs, and desktop/mobile layouts. Use subtle lighting and interaction feedback. Choose between illustrated depth and full 3D after reviewing an early visual prototype. Night mode, extra animations, and hidden interactions can follow.
